Research Domain

Where the Science Lives

From the literature that shaped our thinking to the exact gap that demanded a new kind of answer - the intellectual foundations of EmoSL, laid bare.

Literature Survey

Shoulders of Giants —
and the Gaps They Left

A survey of the existing field reveals a consistent pattern: extraordinary technical achievement applied to a narrow demographic slice of humanity.

Facial Emotion Recognition (FER)

Ekman & Friesen's foundational FACS framework established six universal emotions, but built almost entirely on Western subjects. When applied to South Asian populations, recognition accuracy drops 15-30%, particularly around expressions of surprise and disgust absent from the training vocabulary.

Computer Vision

Hashtag Recommendation

Existing hashtag systems are neither emotion-aware nor bilingual. They cannot generate hashtags in both Sinhala and English. For Sri Lankans who naturally code-switch between the two languages in everyday digital expression, this is a capability that simply does not exist anywhere in the current landscape.

Hashtag · NLP

Multilingual Sentiment Analysis

mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa perform well on high-resource languages, but Sinhala is low-resource by every metric. Annotated corpora for emotion classification in Sinhala are near-nonexistent, making global models genuinely unreliable for Sri Lankan social media text, especially with emoji-laden Singlish expressions.

NLP

Speech Emotion Recognition (SER)

State-of-the-art SER systems - openSMILE, wav2vec 2.0 - clock 80%+ accuracy on English and German corpora. Sri Lankan speech, with its distinct Sinhala tonal patterns, prosodic rhythms and dialect variation, has zero representation in any existing SER benchmark dataset.

Research Gap

The Silence in the Data

Global Emotion AI isn't broken - it simply wasn't built for Sri Lankans. That distinction matters, because the fix isn't a patch. It's an entirely new foundation.

GAP — 01

No Efficient FER Architecture for Local Data

No efficient facial emotion recognition architecture exists that can make meaningful use of the small, localised datasets available for Sri Lankan subjects. Global models demand massive datasets - leaving local data stranded with no practical way to leverage it for culturally accurate emotion recognition.

GAP — 02

Code-Switching Blindness

Sri Lankans routinely compose messages that move between Sinhala and English within a single sentence. No existing NLP model has a framework for this kind of fluid multilingual emotional expression - each language gets processed in isolation, destroying the very meaning that lives in the transitions.

GAP — 03

Emoji Ambiguity Across Cultures

The same emoji carries fundamentally different emotional weight in different cultures. Every existing emoji-emotion mapping was derived from Western usage patterns and doesn't reflect how Sri Lankans deploy emoji as emotional shorthand - making current text analysis systematically misread the tone of Sri Lankan posts.

GAP — 04

No Unified Emotion Ecosystem

Even where individual tools exist for one modality (facial or text) no integrated platform combines all four channels (face, text, hashtag, speech) into a coherent, culturally grounded experience. The gap isn't just in each model. It's in the absence of a system that brings them together.

Research Problem

The Question That Drives Everything

"How can a multimodal AI system - spanning facial expression, natural language, emoji and speech - be designed, trained and deployed to accurately recognise and respond to the emotional states of Sri Lankan social media users, within a culturally coherent mobile-first application?"

Why This Matters

Misclassified emotions aren't just an accuracy problem - they lead to broken content recommendations, disconnected user experiences and a platform that treats its users as someone they're not. For 7.2 million Sri Lankans, that's the daily reality.

Research Objectives

What We Set Out to Prove

01

Design an Efficient Facial Emotion Recognition Model

Build a facial emotion recognition model that actually works for Sri Lankans, making maximum use of the small localised datasets that exist for this population, rather than demanding the massive corpora global models rely on.

02

Design Hashtag-Based Recommendation Logic

Build an emotion-aware bilingual hashtag generation system that dynamically producing relevant hashtags in both English and Sinhala, derived from the detected emotional state, bridging the code-switching reality of Sri Lankan digital expression.

03

Develop Multilingual Emotion Classification

Create an NLP pipeline that detects emotion in Sinhala, English and code-switched combinations, including the emoji layer that carries its own independent emotional grammar distinct from the surrounding text.

04

Develop Speech Emotion Valency Detection

Develop an acoustic model that identifies positive or negative emotional valence in Sri Lankan speech, accounting for regional prosodic variation unique to Sinhala dialects and the tonal patterns absent from global SER benchmarks.

Methodology

How We're Building the Answer

A four-stream parallel research approach - each stream independent, each feeding into one unified mobile architecture at the point of convergence.

Phase 01

Data Sourcing & Preprocessing

All four research streams rely on publicly available datasets. No manual data collection was conducted. Existing datasets were carefully sourced, cleaned and preprocessed by normalising facial expression corpora, curating multilingual social media text samples in Sinhala and English and preparing Sinhala speech audio for acoustic feature extraction. The focus was on extracting maximum signal from available data through rigorous preprocessing pipelines.

Phase 02

Model Development

The FER model was first trained on a large publicly available facial expression dataset to establish a strong baseline, then smoothly fine-tuned on a Sri Lankan dataset, transferring global knowledge to local cultural nuance without starting from scratch. The hashtag recommendation engine was developed as a vision-language transformer mapping visual facial emotion signals to bilingual hashtag outputs in both English and Sinhala. The multilingual emotion detector was built by fine-tuning mBERT on a Singlish text corpus with emoji annotations. The speech valency model was trained using MFCC and mel-spectrogram features extracted from Sinhala audio recordings.

Phase 03

System Integration

All four models were deployed as independent inference services on Google Cloud Platform, connected through a Node.js/Express orchestration layer that handles authentication, routing and user management. The Flutter mobile frontend brings everything together by providing camera, microphone and text input capabilities within a single cohesive application experience.

Phase 04

Evaluation & Validation

Cross-validation of each model on held-out Sri Lankan test sets. User acceptance testing with the target demographic (ages 20-39). Performance benchmarking against global baseline models to quantify the cultural accuracy improvement achieved through localised fine-tuning and architecture design.
